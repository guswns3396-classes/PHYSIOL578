The Hippo pathway scaffold protein RASSF1A was originally identified as a tumor suppressor in lung and breast cancers. Methylation of the RASSF1A gene has been widely associated with poor patient outcomes. To uncover the signaling pathways perturbed by RASSF1A methylation, an unbiased RNA-seq screening approach was performed in several lung and breast cancer cell lines. Interestingly, the TGF$\beta$ signaling pathway was found to be significantly affected in response to RASSF1A deficiency. Based on these analyses, a hypothesis is formed that RASSF1A may function as a signaling link between the Hippo and TGF$\beta$ signaling pathways.

\section{
    Please describe both canonical and non-canonical TGF$\beta$ signaling pathways (0.5 point)
}
In the canonical pathway, TGF-$\beta$ binds a complex of type II and type I serine/threonine kinase receptors.
The type II receptor phosphorylates the type I receptor, which then phosphorylates the receptor-regulated SMADs, SMAD2 and SMAD3.
Phosphorylated SMAD2/3 associate with the co-SMAD SMAD4, enter the nucleus, and work with transcriptional cofactors to activate or repress specific genes.
SMAD7 provides negative feedback by inhibiting receptor-mediated activation of SMAD2/3.

Noncanonical TGF-$\beta$ signaling includes the ERK, JNK/p38, and PI3K-Akt pathways.
In the ERK pathway, TGF-$\beta$ receptors activate Ras-Raf-MEK1/2, leading to ERK1/2 phosphorylation and altered cellular activity and gene expression.
In the JNK/p38 pathway, TRAF6 activates TAK1, which promotes JNK and p38 phosphorylation.
In the PI3K-Akt pathway, PI3K promotes Akt phosphorylation.

These pathways regulate processes such as survival, proliferation, migration, and EMT.

\section{
    Please describe the classical Hippo pathway in a mammalian system (0.5 point)
}
Unlike many signaling pathways, Hippo signaling has no clear ligand-receptor pair.
Still, the pathway involves LATS1/2 inhibiting YAP by phosphorylating it.
This prevents YAP from going into the nucleus to partner with TEAD to activate growth.
Other regulators of the signaling are AMPK, which can directly inhibit YAP by phosphorylating it,
PKA, which can indirectly inhibit YAP, and phosphatidic acid, which can indirectly promote YAP by inhibiting LATS1/2.
